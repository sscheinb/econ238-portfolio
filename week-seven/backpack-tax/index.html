Backpack Tax: Make Hikers and Campers Help Fund Wildlife

Read the proposition below, then cast an anonymous vote. It takes about two minutes.

The ballot question

Shall Congress, starting January 1, 2028, add a 5 percent federal excise tax, paid by manufacturers, on new hiking, camping, climbing and paddling gear, excluding hunting and fishing gear already taxed, with all revenue deposited in a fund for state wildlife agencies to protect at-risk species, and the Government Accountability Office reporting after three years on revenue, species funded, and effects on gear prices and sales?

A YES vote means the 5 percent tax on new hiking, camping, climbing and paddling gear starts January 1, 2028, and every dollar goes to state wildlife agencies. A NO vote keeps today's system, where wildlife agencies rely mainly on hunters and anglers.

Vote: YES / NO / YES, IF…

What's the deal

We are buying money to protect wildlife before species become endangered. Today license fees and excise taxes paid by hunters and anglers make up about 80 percent of state wildlife agency revenue. We give up cheaper gear: a $250 tent would cost about $12.50 more if the tax is fully passed on. Gear buyers pay at the register, and makers and stores may sell less. State agencies benefit, along with the roughly 12,000 species they call at risk. A Resources for the Future study estimated a 5 percent tax on outdoor equipment would raise about $3.2 billion a year, about $10 per household. That is more than the $1.4 billion a year proposed in the Recovering America's Wildlife Act.

Best case for YES

This is the oldest idea in American conservation. In 1937 sportsmen essentially encouraged taxing themselves through the Pittman-Robertson Act, and its 11 percent tax on guns and ammunition has raised about $16 billion. Yet only 14 million Americans hunt and 40 million fish, while 148 million watch wildlife and pay no special fee. A hiker enjoys the same elk and the same trails. Georgia and Texas already send part of their sales tax on outdoor gear to parks and conservation, so the idea works in practice. At about $10 per household a year the cost is small, and it asks everyone who values wildlife to share the bill that hunters and anglers have carried alone.

Best case for NO

Gear is how people get outdoors, and a tax makes it cost more for beginners and students, who can least afford it. The Resources for the Future study found a tax like this is slightly regressive: the lowest income fifth pays about 2.6 times the share of income that the top fifth does. It also found demand for gear is very sensitive to price, so sales could fall and take revenue with them. Hunters and anglers take animals, so their fees compensate for that, while hiking takes nothing. Watching wildlife is also not the same as hiking, so many taxpayers would pay for conservation they never use. Gear makers and stores would absorb part of the loss.

Where is the body buried

The weakest assumption is that the tax would raise what the study says. That estimate comes from one economic model of general outdoor gear, not hiking gear specifically, and the 5 percent rate is my choice. The result exceeds the $1.4 billion proposed in the Recovering America's Wildlife Act, so a lower rate might do. The 148 million wildlife watchers are not the same people as hikers, so that comparison is loose. I also found no evidence yet that the money would actually save species.

Why isn't this already happening

Pittman-Robertson passed because sportsmen backed it and essentially asked to be taxed. Hikers and birdwatchers are tens of millions of people with no comparable lobby, each paying a small amount, so nobody fights hard for it, while gear makers and stores fight against price increases. The Recovering America's Wildlife Act has been introduced repeatedly since 2016 and still has no agreed source of funds. A federal tax needs Congress, so states moved first: Georgia and Texas redirected existing sales taxes by constitutional amendment, which avoids a new tax but needs a vote and only helps one state.
