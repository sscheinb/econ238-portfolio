Feed the Ocean Our Leftovers

Paying steelmakers to turn their waste into fish

Read the full project, with calculations and sources →

How it works

  1. Steel millMakes slag, an iron-rich rock it pays to dispose of
  2. ShipCarries prepared slag to the coast
  3. Iron-poor fishing groundRun by a local fishing co-op that owns the extra catch
  4. Plankton bloomIron is the missing nutrient
  5. More fishCo-op sells them and shares the value

The numbers

190–290 Mtof steel slag made worldwide every year
2.3 kgof usable iron per ton of extra fish (my estimate)
0.05%of slag iron must be usable to cover slag, shipping and the cost of catching the fish (0.5% if only 1 in 10 extra fish is caught)

Assumes slag is 15% iron, fish are worth about $1,720 per ton, slag costs $5 per ton plus about $22.50 shipping, and catching the fish costs half their value. Assumptions are listed in the full project.

The economic benefit: a 1,000 ton pilot

$377kleft for the fishing co-op each year if plankton use 1% of slag iron
0.8 yrsto pay back a $300,000 setup in that case
−$122kloss per year if plankton use only 0.1%, so the pilot is mainly buying information

At 100,000 tons a year the fixed cost spreads out, and break-even falls to about 0.07% usable iron. All dollar figures are modeled from my assumptions, not measured prices.

Why it is not as crazy as it sounds

Nippon Steel already uses slag, soil and humus to bring back seaweed beds at Mashike, Japan. Kelp grew well 30 meters from the units after six months, and the effect held after two and three years. The proven scale is small, which is why this idea starts with pilots.

Who gains
  • Steelmakers: no disposal cost
  • Fishing co-ops: bigger catch
  • Shipping: more cargo
What could go wrong
  • Toxic algae blooms and oxygen loss
  • Cement and road builders lose a cheap input
  • Banned outside research since 2013
  • Slag iron may not dissolve well
