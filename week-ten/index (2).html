The University of Rochester should have a gate

A proposed MOAT, in the spirit of the Faroese grindadráp

What Rochester should do

Make one weed-out course official in every major, such as organic chemistry for biology and chemistry, or a proof-based course for math. Earn at least a B or switch majors. Say it publicly and make no exceptions.

Why it works like a whale hunt

The grindadráp is costly and controversial, and the cost is the point. Surviving the gate is a shared ordeal that binds each major together, and it tells people who want a degree without the work that this is not the place. It also rations crowded majors like computer science by effort, not by who got in first.

Who will fight it

Administrators worried about retention and rankings. Critics who say it punishes first generation students and those from weaker high schools. And anyone tempted to cheat in the one course that matters.

The cost, and who pays

The most visible cost is lost time and extra tuition for students forced to switch majors. Probably not the largest: the bigger loss may be capable students who would have succeeded with support but leave or transfer instead.

The softer version

One retake with tutoring keeps most of the signal and cuts the equity cost. Even so, it is easy to copy, so it only works if Rochester commits publicly.
